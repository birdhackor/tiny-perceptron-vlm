20:                                                                                                              Abstract
21:                                                                          Knowledge Distillation (KD) is a promising technique for reducing the high com-
22:                                                                          putational demand of large language models (LLMs). However, previous KD
23:                                                                          methods are primarily applied to white-box classification models or training small
24:                                                                          models to imitate black-box model APIs like ChatGPT. How to effectively dis-
25:                                                                          till the knowledge of white-box LLMs into small models is still under-explored,
26:                                                                          which becomes more important with the prosperity of open-source LLMs. In this
27:                                                                          work, we propose a KD approach that distills LLMs into smaller language mod-
28:                                                                          els. We first replace the forward Kullback-Leibler divergence (KLD) objective in
29:                                                                          the standard KD approaches with reverse KLD, which is more suitable for KD
30:                                                                          on generative language models, to prevent the student model from overestimating
31:                                                                          the low-probability regions of the teacher distribution. Then, we derive an effec-
32:                                                                          tive on-policy optimization approach to learn this objective. The student models
33:                                                                          are named M INI LLM. Extensive experiments in the instruction-following set-
34:                                                                          ting show that M INI LLM generates more precise responses with higher overall
35:                                                                          quality, lower exposure bias, better calibration, and higher long-text generation
36:                                                                          performance than the baselines. Our method is scalable for different model fami-
37:                                                                          lies with 120M to 13B parameters. Our code, data, and model checkpoints can be
38:                                                                          found in https://github.com/microsoft/LMOps/tree/main/minillm.

68: 1   Introduction
69: 
70: With the rapid development of large language models (LLMs; BMR+ 20, HZD+ 21, BHA+ 21,
71: CND+ 22, Ope23), a common technique to reduce their high computational resource demand is
72: knowledge distillation (KD; HVD15), where we train a small student model with supervision from
73: a large teacher model. Two categories of KD are commonly applied: black-box KD, where only
74: the teacher-generated texts are accessible, and white-box KD, where the teacher model’s output dis-
75: tribution or intermediate hidden states are also available [JBMD21]. Recently, black-box KD has
76: shown promising results in fine-tuning small models on the prompt-response pairs generated by
77: LLM APIs [TGZ+ 23, CLL+ 23, WWZ+ 23, PLH+ 23]. With the emergence of more open-source
78: LLMs [ZRG+ 22, TLI+ 23], white-box KD becomes more valuable for both research communities
79: and industry sectors because student models receive better signals from the output distribution and
80: hidden states of teacher models, thereby potentially resulting in higher performance. However,
81: white-box KD approaches are mostly studied for small (< 1B parameters) language understanding
82: models [SDCW19, WWD+ 20], while white-box KD for LLMs is yet to be explored.
83: In this work, we investigate white-box KD of LLMs where the output distribution of the teacher
84: model is available. We argue that the standard KD objectives [KR16, SST+ 20, CLL+ 23, TGZ+ 23]
85: are sub-optimal for LLMs that perform tasks in a generative manner. Given the teacher distri-
86: bution p(y|x) and the student distribution qθ (y|x) parameterized by θ, standard KD objectives
87: (including several variants for sequence-level models) essentially minimize the approximated for-
88: ward Kullback-Leibler divergence (KLD) between the teacher and the student distribution, termed
89: as KL[p||qθ ], which forces qθ to cover all modes of p. For text classification tasks, KL[p||qθ ] works
90: well because the output space usually consists of a finite number of classes such that both p(y|x) and
91: qθ (y|x) have few modes. However, for open-ended text generation tasks, which is usually the case
92: of LLM applications, the output spaces are much more complex and p(y|x) can contain many more
93: modes than what qθ (y|x) can express due to the limited model capacity. Minimizing forward KLD
94: causes qθ to assign unreasonably high probabilities to the void regions of p [MG19] and produces
95: very unlikely samples under p during free-run generation [Hus15].

122: standard KD baselines on all the datasets and scales up well from 120M to 13B models (see Figure
123: 1). More analysis shows that M INI LLM yields lower exposure bias, better calibration, and higher
124: long response generation performance, with neglectable loss of diversity.

154: Though widely used, KL[p||qθ ] tends to overestimate the void regions of p in text generation tasks
155: when qθ is insufficiently expressive [JKH+ 23]. KD for LLMs fits the case because LLMs perform
156: tasks in a generative manner, such that the low-capacity student models cannot perfectly imitate the
157: complex text generation distribution of the teacher models or humans.

170: Minimizing reverse KLD has been shown to cause the mode-seeking behavior in generative mod-
171: eling [Hus15, NCT16, CDP+ 18, LPSK23], where qθ assigns high probabilities to p’s large modes
172: and ignore the small ones (illustrated in a toy experiment in Figure 2). In this work, we first study
173: this property for KD of LLMs in text generation. Minimizing forward KLD causes qθ to place large
174: probability masses on the zero-probability regions of p, corresponding to the generation of low-
175: quality text in practice, while reverse KLD focuses on p’s major modes, which is crucial to ensure
176: the correctness and faithfulness of text generation. As illustrated in Figure 3, unlike sequence-level
177: KD that minimizes forward KLD [KR16, TGZ+ 23], M INI LLM that minimizes reverse KLD does
178: not force qθ to fit all y sampled from the teacher distribution p. Instead, it encourages the student
179: to generate samples preferred by the teacher within its own capacities, which is more possible to
180: achieve. Interestingly, we also find another perspective of understanding M INI LLM motivated by
181: Inverse Reinforcement Learning [ZMB+ 08]. We present the related derivation in Appendix A.1.
