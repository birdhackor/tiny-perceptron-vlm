98:     Based on our estimated compute-optimal frontier, we predict that for the compute budget used
99: to train Gopher, an optimal model should be 4 times smaller, while being training on 4 times more
100: tokens. We verify this by training a more compute-optimal 70B model, called Chinchilla, on 1.4 trillion
101: tokens. Not only does Chinchilla outperform its much larger counterpart, Gopher, but its reduced
102: model size reduces inference cost considerably and greatly facilitates downstream uses on smaller
103: hardware. The energy cost of a large language model is amortized through its usage for inference an
104: fine-tuning. The benefits of a more optimally trained smaller model, therefore, extend beyond the
105: immediate benefits of its improved performance.

1877: E. Curvature of the FLOP-loss frontier
1878: We observe that as models increase there is a curvature in the FLOP-minimal loss frontier. This means
1879: that projections from very small models lead to different predictions than those from larger models.
1880: In Figure A5 we show linear fits using the first, middle, and final third of frontier-points. In this work,
1881: we do not take this in to account and we leave this as interesting future work as it suggests that even
1882: smaller models may be optimal for large FLOP budgets.
