With the choice r=k (which is crucial when k is large because of
numerical cancellation) the integrand equals ek at the dominant points
θ=0,2⁢π, and in combination with the factor k−k in front of the
integral sign this gives a rough approximation to 1/k!. The choice r=k is
motivated by saddle-point analysis; see §2.4(iv) or examples in
§3.5(ix). As explained in §§3.5(i) and
3.5(ix) the composite trapezoidal rule can be very efficient for
computing integrals with analytic periodic integrands.
