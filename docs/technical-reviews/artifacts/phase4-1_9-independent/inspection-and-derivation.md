## Independent algebra and axes

The parameter axis is scalar w. L=(w−3)^2 is an illustrative loss with arbitrary loss units, not nat/bit cross-entropy. L≥0 and reaches its minimum 0 at w=3. At w=1 it is 4. For a positive displacement d from 1:

`L(1+d)−L(1) = (−2+d)^2−4 = −4d+d^2`, hence `(ΔL)/(Δw)=−4+d`. Therefore d=0.1 yields 3.61, ΔL=−0.39 and quotient −3.9; d=0.01 yields 3.9601, ΔL=−0.0399 and quotient −3.99. As d→0, the quotient tends to −4.

For arbitrary w, `[L(w+h)−L(w−h)]/(2h) = [((w−3)+h)^2−((w−3)−h)^2]/(2h) = 2(w−3)` when h≠0 in exact arithmetic. This quadratic has an exact centered difference for every nonzero h; the source's general warning that a large h *may* miss local sensitivity is not an observed large-h failure of this particular quadratic. NIST DLMF §3.4(i), equation 3.4.6 at t=0 yields `[f(x0+h)−f(x0−h)]/(2h)` plus the derivative interpolation remainder; equations 3.4.3–4 supply the smoothness-dependent remainder, which vanishes for a quadratic. A separate sin check illustrates the general limitation (h=1 gives 0.84147098 rather than derivative 1; h=.001 gives .9999998333).

At w=1 and h=.001, left/right loss values are 4.004001 and 3.996001. ΔL from left to right is −.008000 across Δw=.002, so the quotient is −4 loss units per w unit. It is not −8 and does not divide by h alone. For the one-sided move from 1 to 1.001, ΔL=−.003999, while L′(1)Δw=−.004. The error is exactly 1e−6 loss units (.02500625% of the exact change), consistent with the source's use of 約. A distant move 1→6 changes L from 4→9, proving that the negative local derivative does not guarantee improvement from arbitrary positive jumps.

At w=5, derivative is +4, left point 4.999 is nearer target 3 and its loss 3.996001 is less than the right loss 4.004001. At w=3 both losses are .000001 and the derivative is zero; in this specific convex quadratic this is the minimum. No general claim that all zero gradients are minima is made.

Exact decimal identities were checked at Decimal precision 40. Actual binary64 fence executions for w=1/5/3 were compared to −4/+4/0 with absolute tolerance 2e−12, far smaller than the displayed precision. The biggest error was about 1.34e−12 at w=5. In all executions w retained its initial value. These are three scalar demonstrations; there is no dataset, token/sample average or training-step denominator. For perturbation output errors ε+ and ε−, dividing the loss difference adds error `(ε+−ε−)/(2h)`, which can grow as h shrinks; Python 3.13 official floating-point documentation explains binary representation and per-operation rounding error. An additional h=1e−16 scalar check returns 0 instead of −4, illustrating this numerical limitation without substituting it for the official explanation.
