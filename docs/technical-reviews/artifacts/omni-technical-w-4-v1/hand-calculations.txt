1. Finite equally likely cards
The sample space has four distinguishable cards. Each has probability 1/4.
The three cat cards are disjoint outcomes, so P(cat) = 1/4+1/4+1/4 = 3/4.
P(dog) = 1/4. Their union is the whole sample space, so 3/4+1/4 = 1.
If cat and dog are the exhaustive, mutually exclusive candidate events,
P(cat)+P(dog) = 1 is required. Assigning 0.9 to both gives 9/10+9/10 = 18/10 = 1.8,
which exceeds 1 by 0.8. All fractions and decimal additions here are exact.
This is a finite categorical example; no continuous-outcome interpretation is used.

2. Natural logarithm, sign and endpoints
The real exponential exp(a) is positive, strictly increasing, and exp(0)=1.
Its inverse ln(p) therefore has domain p>0. If 0<p<1, then a=ln(p)<0;
consequently -ln(p)>0. At p=1, -ln(1)=0.
For 0<p<q<=1, ln(p)<ln(q), hence -ln(p)>-ln(q).
Continuity at p=1 gives lim(p->1) -ln(p)=0.
For any proposed finite upper bound M>0, choosing 0<p<exp(-M) gives -ln(p)>M.
Thus the loss is unbounded as p approaches zero from above. At p=0 the real
logarithm is undefined; the extended-value loss is +infinity, not a finite cost.
The Python exception is checked separately in cpu-output.json.

3. Product rule
For p1,p2>0, write a=ln(p1), b=ln(p2), so p1=exp(a), p2=exp(b).
Then p1*p2=exp(a+b); taking the inverse gives ln(p1*p2)=a+b.
Multiplying by -1 gives -ln(p1*p2)=-ln(p1)-ln(p2).
Independence is not needed for this algebra. It is needed if p1,p2 are marginal
event probabilities and their product is claimed to be the joint probability.

4. Independent two-question example
Under the stated independence assumption,
P(both correct)=(1/2)*(1/2)=1/4 exactly.
The exact real-valued loss identity is -ln(1/4)=2*ln(2).
The displayed relation 1.386=0.693+0.693 uses rounded decimal values.
Unrounded logarithm approximations and the original program are checked by
actual CPU execution in cpu-output.json, not claimed as hand-computed decimals.
The algebraic identity is exact over real numbers; arbitrary floating-point
evaluations need not be bitwise identical.

5. Summing positions and taking a mean
For positive p_i, induction on the product rule yields
-ln(product_i p_i)=sum_i -ln(p_i).
For n equally weighted positions the mean is (1/n)*sum_i -ln(p_i).
Real language-model joint likelihoods use probabilities conditional on prior
tokens; summing their logs does not assert that text positions are independent.
Zero factors require the extended-infinite convention. Direct sums of log
probabilities avoid forming a product that may underflow in finite precision.

6. Softmax and categorical cross entropy
For finite real scores z_j, let p_j=exp(z_j)/sum_k exp(z_k).
Every exp(z_j)>0 and the denominator is positive; summing numerators gives
sum_j p_j=1 in real arithmetic. For a hard class label y and unit weight,
the per-position cross-entropy term is -ln(p_y).
With reduction='mean', no ignored positions, no class weights, and no label
smoothing, the result is the arithmetic mean across the positions.
This derivation does not promise exact floating-point normalization, statistical
calibration, training quality, or the same denominator for weighted targets.
