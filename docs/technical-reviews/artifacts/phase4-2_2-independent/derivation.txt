Independent 2.2 derivation
IDs are a chosen dictionary convention: 1→[1,10], 2→[2,20], 3→[3,30].
Two ID rows of three positions have [B,C]=[2,3]. Lookup appends D=2 features: [2,3,2].
Flatten each row keeping position then feature order: [1,10,2,20,3,30]; [3,30,2,20,1,10].
B*C*D=2*3*2=12 elements, reshape(2,-1) means 12/2=6 per row. No arithmetic combines cells.
For positive B,C,D, flattening gives [B,C*D], and reshape back recovers the original logical grouping.
The first row exercise [2,1,3] yields [2,20,1,10,3,30], while the second row is unchanged.
Every original and exercise row sums to 66; sums cannot recover the shown permutations.
A hypothetical recipe with weights [1,0,0,0,-1,0] gives 1-3=-2 and 3-1=2, so a position-specific affine layer can distinguish them. The section does not instantiate this layer.
With D=2, C=3 has six inputs but C=5 has ten; a fixed six-input Linear cannot accept ten directly. Changing B does not change in_features.
The feature values are arbitrary tracking numbers with no units or semantic magnitude claim; counts use elements, not independent information dimensions.
All arithmetic here is exact over small integers representable in float32; no rounding tolerance is needed.
