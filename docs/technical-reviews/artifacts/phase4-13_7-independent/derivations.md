# Independent derivations for 13.7

`4` is U+0034, 1 UTF-8 byte. `。` is U+3002, 3 UTF-8 bytes. ByteTokenizer assigns 1 token to each byte. Eight `4。` pairs therefore contain 8×(1+3)=32 body tokens; adding one EOS answer target gives 33. The short answer gives 1+1=2. Two pairs give 2×4=8 body tokens and 9 answer targets. The ASCII string `4; answer complete` contains 1+1+1+6+1+8=18 body tokens and 19 with EOS. Role and question positions are ignored, and padding is excluded from both counts and scores.

For each answer y, S_policy(y)=Σ_t log p_policy(y_t | x,y_<t), where t runs over all valid answer targets, including the final EOS in this experiment. S_ref(y) uses exactly that same answer and target mask. Relative DPO margin is M=[S_policy(chosen)−S_ref(chosen)]−[S_policy(rejected)−S_ref(rejected)]. These are natural logs of full sequence probabilities. Dividing each of the four scores by its own answer's length changes the objective whenever the two answer lengths differ. Example (chosen length 2, rejected length 4): policy scores (-4,-6), reference scores (-6,-8). The sum margin is (-4+6)−(-6+8)=0. The mean margin is (-2+1.5)−(-3+2)=0.5. At beta=.1, the DPO loss changes from log(2) to -log(sigmoid(.05)). This is a mathematical comparison, not a finding about a trained model.

A 2-outcome distribution can give probability sigmoid(theta) to the long duplicate and sigmoid(-theta) to the short answer, with an equal fixed reference. If the preference label chooses the long duplicate, the DPO logit is beta×theta; at theta=0, d(loss)/d(theta)=-beta/2=-.05. Reference subtraction therefore cannot, by itself, prohibit fitting a preference label correlated with answer length. No parameters are updated in the CPU demonstration. The possibility and mature observed phenomenon are supported separately by the original length-bias paper.

If the frozen reference is fixed across an update, the change of relative margin is ΔM=ΔS_policy(chosen)−ΔS_policy(rejected). This can be positive even if both scores fall, provided the rejected score falls more. The saved validation example gives -0.06612968444824219−(-115.8443603515625)=115.77823066711426. The chosen answer probability ratio is exp(-.06612968444824219)=.9360094705486112; ranking improvement does not establish a rise in that answer's probability or generation accuracy.

The task-dependent long example states two groups with two blocks each and a total of four. It contains an explanation missing from the bare digit, but it violates an instruction to output only a number. A preference label conditions on the request, not merely on the answer length. Matching lengths and contrasting completeness/repetition are design recommendations to reduce ambiguity about what a preference pair rewards; they are not claims of a measured universal cure.
