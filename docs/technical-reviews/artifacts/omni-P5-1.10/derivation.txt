1. Local derivative, not division of function values
For f(u)=u^2, [f(u+h)-f(u)]/h = [2*u*h+h^2]/h = 2*u+h.
Taking h toward zero gives f'(u)=2*u. At u=6 this is 12.
The quotient f(6)/6=36/6=6 is different; dL/du does not mean L/u.

2. Base composition
u=2*w, L=u^2, so L=4*w^2.
At w=3: u=6, L=36, du/dw=2, dL/du=12.
dL/dw=(dL/du)*(du/dw)=12*2=24, also d(4*w^2)/dw=8*w=24.
Forward values (3,6,36) and local derivatives (2,12,24) are different quantities.

3. Finite step and first-order approximation
For h=0.001, delta_u=2*h=0.002 exactly in real/decimal arithmetic.
L(3+h)-L(3)=4*((3+h)^2-3^2)=24*h+4*h^2
=0.024+0.000004=0.024004.
The first-order prediction is (dL/du)*delta_u=12*0.002=0.024.
The exact forward-difference quotient is 0.024004/0.001=24.004.
The derivative at w=3 is 24; the text explicitly uses approximations for the
finite step and sensitivity estimate, so these are consistent statements.

4. Exercise

At w=3: u=9, L=81, du/dw=3, dL/du=2*9=18.
dL/dw=18*3=54, also d(9*w^2)/dw=18*w=54.
54/24=2.25=(3/2)^2, not 1.5, because both local factors change.
The display constant and assertion must change along with the calculation,

5. Multiple paths (optional note)
For differentiable L=f(u,v), u=g(w), v=k(w), the first-order change is
delta_L=(partial_L/partial_u)*g'(w)*delta_w
       +(partial_L/partial_v)*k'(w)*delta_w + o(delta_w).
Therefore the contributions of both paths add in dL/dw.
Reviewer-created example: u=2*w, v=3*w, L=u^2+v^2.
At w=3 the path contributions are 24 and 54; their sum is 78.
This polynomial identity is not a training or generalization experiment.
