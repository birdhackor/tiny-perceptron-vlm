# Independent assessment of the hand-written 8.1 task

The request is to explain 2+2 using an everyday analogy. The section chooses
two independent success conditions: the number must be correct, and the
objects and grouping of the analogy must correspond to the addition problem.
This is a deliberately small binary rubric, not an automated metric of humor.

Arithmetic and units: two pairs of chopsticks contain 2 sticks per pair, hence
2 sticks + 2 sticks = 4 sticks. The sum is exact integer arithmetic (tolerance
zero), with two groups as the grouping axis. “4” satisfies the numeric
condition while omitting the requested explanation. The chopstick sentence
gives the number, the two pairs and their combined number, so satisfies both
specified conditions. “5, numbers flow like starlight” fails the sum and has
no correspondence between two groups of two and their combination. A sea
sentence beginning with 4 retains numeric correctness but gives neither two
groups nor a combining operation; it fails the second specified condition.

These are three chosen responses, not three random model samples. The flags
are supplied by a person. There is no model-generated denominator, accuracy
estimate, style-scale score, training, gradient, or semantic detector in the
original fence. Changing text alone leaves the supplied flag unchanged.
The exercise requires explicitly changing that flag after human inspection.

The rubric does not test punctuation, output serialization, arbitrary poetic
quality, general arithmetic skill, or broad metaphor competence. Likewise,
the saved style experiment's substring rule is a different, narrower check
than this conceptual grouping rubric. The current supplementary paragraph
explicitly calls out that difference. Its empirical support is the archived
style result re-scored using the source revision recorded in that result.
