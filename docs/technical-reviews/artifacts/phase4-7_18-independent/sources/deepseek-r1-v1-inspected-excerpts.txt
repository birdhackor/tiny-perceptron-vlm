L231: Reinforcement learning has demonstrated significant effectiveness in reasoning tasks, as ev-

L277: 2.2.2. Reward Modeling
L278: 
L279: The reward is the source of the training signal, which decides the optimization direction of RL.
L280: To train DeepSeek-R1-Zero, we adopt a rule-based reward system that mainly consists of two
L281: types of rewards:
L282:    • Accuracy rewards: The accuracy reward model evaluates whether the response is correct.
L283:      For example, in the case of math problems with deterministic results, the model is required
L284:      to provide the final answer in a specified format (e.g., within a box), enabling reliable
L285:      rule-based verification of correctness. Similarly, for LeetCode problems, a compiler can be
L286:      used to generate feedback based on predefined test cases.
L287:    • Format rewards: In addition to the accuracy reward model, we employ a format reward
L288:      model that enforces the model to put its thinking process between ‘<think>’ and ‘</think>’
L289:      tags.
L290: We do not apply the outcome or process neural reward model in developing DeepSeek-R1-Zero,
L291: because we find that the neural reward model may suffer from reward hacking in the large-scale
L292: reinforcement learning process, and retraining the reward model needs additional training
L293: resources and it complicates the whole training pipeline.
L294: 
L295: 2.2.3. Training Template
L296: 
L297: To train DeepSeek-R1-Zero, we begin by designing a straightforward template that guides
L298: the base model to adhere to our specified instructions. As depicted in Table 1, this template
L299: requires DeepSeek-R1-Zero to first produce a reasoning process, followed by the final answer.
L300: We intentionally limit our constraints to this structural format, avoiding any content-specific
L301: biases—such as mandating reflective reasoning or promoting particular problem-solving strate-
L302: gies—to ensure that we can accurately observe the model’s natural progression during the RL
L303: process.

L431: 2.3. DeepSeek-R1: Reinforcement Learning with Cold Start
L432: 
L433: Inspired by the promising results of DeepSeek-R1-Zero, two natural questions arise: 1) Can
L434: reasoning performance be further improved or convergence accelerated by incorporating a small
L435: amount of high-quality data as a cold start? 2) How can we train a user-friendly model that
L436: not only produces clear and coherent Chains of Thought (CoT) but also demonstrates strong
L437: general capabilities? To address these questions, we design a pipeline to train DeepSeek-R1. The
L438: pipeline consists of four stages, outlined as follows.
L439: 
L440: 2.3.1. Cold Start
L441: 
L442: Unlike DeepSeek-R1-Zero, to prevent the early unstable cold start phase of RL training from
L443: the base model, for DeepSeek-R1 we construct and collect a small amount of long CoT data
L444: to fine-tune the model as the initial RL actor. To collect such data, we have explored several
L445: approaches: using few-shot prompting with a long CoT as an example, directly prompting
L446: models to generate detailed answers with reflection and verification, gathering DeepSeek-R1-
L447: Zero outputs in a readable format, and refining the results through post-processing by human
L448: annotators.
L449:    In this work, we collect thousands of cold-start data to fine-tune the DeepSeek-V3-Base as
L450: the starting point for RL. Compared to DeepSeek-R1-Zero, the advantages of cold start data
L451: 
L452: 
L453:                                                      9
L454: include:
L455:    • Readability: A key limitation of DeepSeek-R1-Zero is that its content is often not suitable
L456:      for reading. Responses may mix multiple languages or lack markdown formatting to
L457:      highlight answers for users. In contrast, when creating cold-start data for DeepSeek-R1,
L458:      we design a readable pattern that includes a summary at the end of each response and
L459:      filters out responses that are not reader-friendly. Here, we define the output format as
L460:      |special_token|<reasoning_process>|special_token|<summary>, where the reasoning
L461:      process is the CoT for the query, and the summary is used to summarize the reasoning
L462:      results.
L463:    • Potential: By carefully designing the pattern for cold-start data with human priors, we
L464:      observe better performance against DeepSeek-R1-Zero. We believe the iterative training is
L465:      a better way for reasoning models.
L466: 
L467: 2.3.2. Reasoning-oriented Reinforcement Learning
L468: 
L469: After fine-tuning DeepSeek-V3-Base on the cold start data, we apply the same large-scale
L470: reinforcement learning training process as employed in DeepSeek-R1-Zero. This phase focuses
L471: on enhancing the model’s reasoning capabilities, particularly in reasoning-intensive tasks such
L472: as coding, mathematics, science, and logic reasoning, which involve well-defined problems with
L473: clear solutions. During the training process, we observe that CoT often exhibits language mixing,
L474: particularly when RL prompts involve multiple languages. To mitigate the issue of language
L475: mixing, we introduce a language consistency reward during RL training, which is calculated
L476: as the proportion of target language words in the CoT. Although ablation experiments show
L477: that such alignment results in a slight degradation in the model’s performance, this reward
L478: aligns with human preferences, making it more readable. Finally, we combine the accuracy of
L479: reasoning tasks and the reward for language consistency by directly summing them to form the
L480: final reward. We then apply RL training on the fine-tuned model until it achieves convergence
L481: on reasoning tasks.
L482: 
L483: 2.3.3. Rejection Sampling and Supervised Fine-Tuning
L484: 
L485: When reasoning-oriented RL converges, we utilize the resulting checkpoint to collect SFT
L486: (Supervised Fine-Tuning) data for the subsequent round. Unlike the initial cold-start data, which
L487: primarily focuses on reasoning, this stage incorporates data from other domains to enhance the
L488: model’s capabilities in writing, role-playing, and other general-purpose tasks. Specifically, we
L489: generate the data and fine-tune the model as described below.
L490: 
L491: 
L492: Reasoning data We curate reasoning prompts and generate reasoning trajectories by perform-
L493: ing rejection sampling from the checkpoint from the above RL training. In the previous stage,
L494: we only included data that could be evaluated using rule-based rewards. However, in this stage,
L495: we expand the dataset by incorporating additional data, some of which use a generative reward
L496: model by feeding the ground-truth and model predictions into DeepSeek-V3 for judgment.
L497: Additionally, because the model output is sometimes chaotic and difficult to read, we have
L498: filtered out chain-of-thought with mixed languages, long parapraphs, and code blocks. For
L499: each prompt, we sample multiple responses and retain only the correct ones. In total, we collect
L500: about 600k reasoning related training samples.

L515: 2.3.4. Reinforcement Learning for all Scenarios
L516: 
L517: To further align the model with human preferences, we implement a secondary reinforcement
L518: learning stage aimed at improving the model’s helpfulness and harmlessness while simultane-
L519: ously refining its reasoning capabilities. Specifically, we train the model using a combination
L520: of reward signals and diverse prompt distributions. For reasoning data, we adhere to the
L521: methodology outlined in DeepSeek-R1-Zero, which utilizes rule-based rewards to guide the
L522: learning process in math, code, and logical reasoning domains. For general data, we resort to
L523: reward models to capture human preferences in complex and nuanced scenarios. We build
L524: upon the DeepSeek-V3 pipeline and adopt a similar distribution of preference pairs and train-
L525: ing prompts. For helpfulness, we focus exclusively on the final summary, ensuring that the
L526: assessment emphasizes the utility and relevance of the response to the user while minimizing
L527: interference with the underlying reasoning process. For harmlessness, we evaluate the entire
L528: response of the model, including both the reasoning process and the summary, to identify and
L529: mitigate any potential risks, biases, or harmful content that may arise during the generation
L530: process. Ultimately, the integration of reward signals and diverse data distributions enables us
L531: to train a model that excels in reasoning while prioritizing helpfulness and harmlessness.
L532:

L534: 2.4. Distillation: Empower Small Models with Reasoning Capability
L535: 
L536: To equip more efficient smaller models with reasoning capabilities like DeepSeek-R1, we directly
L537: fine-tuned open-source models like Qwen (Qwen, 2024b) and Llama (AI@Meta, 2024) using
L538: the 800k samples curated with DeepSeek-R1, as detailed in §2.3.3. Our findings indicate that
L539: this straightforward distillation method significantly enhances the reasoning abilities of smaller
L540: models. The base models we use here are Qwen2.5-Math-1.5B, Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Qwen2.5-
L541: 14B, Qwen2.5-32B, Llama-3.1-8B, and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct. We select Llama-3.3 because its
L542: reasoning capability is slightly better than that of Llama-3.1.
L543:     For distilled models, we apply only SFT and do not include an RL stage, even though
L544: incorporating RL could substantially boost model performance. Our primary goal here is to
L545: demonstrate the effectiveness of the distillation technique, leaving the exploration of the RL
L546: stage to the broader research community.
