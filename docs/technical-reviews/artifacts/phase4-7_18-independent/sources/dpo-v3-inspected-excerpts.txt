L19:                                                                                                Abstract
L20: 
L21:                                                       While large-scale unsupervised language models (LMs) learn broad world knowl-
L22:                                                       edge and some reasoning skills, achieving precise control of their behavior is
L23:                                                       difficult due to the completely unsupervised nature of their training. Existing
L24:                                                       methods for gaining such steerability collect human labels of the relative quality of
L25:                                                       model generations and fine-tune the unsupervised LM to align with these prefer-
L26:                                                       ences, often with reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). However,
L27:                                                       RLHF is a complex and often unstable procedure, first fitting a reward model that
L28:                                                       reflects the human preferences, and then fine-tuning the large unsupervised LM
L29:                                                       using reinforcement learning to maximize this estimated reward without drifting
L30:                                                       too far from the original model. In this paper we introduce a new parameterization
L31:                                                       of the reward model in RLHF that enables extraction of the corresponding optimal
L32:                                                       policy in closed form, allowing us to solve the standard RLHF problem with only a
L33:                                                       simple classification loss. The resulting algorithm, which we call Direct Prefer-
L34:                                                       ence Optimization (DPO), is stable, performant, and computationally lightweight,
L35:                                                       eliminating the need for sampling from the LM during fine-tuning or performing
L36:                                                       significant hyperparameter tuning. Our experiments show that DPO can fine-tune
L37:                                                       LMs to align with human preferences as well as or better than existing methods.
L38:                                                       Notably, fine-tuning with DPO exceeds PPO-based RLHF in ability to control sen-
L39:                                                       timent of generations, and matches or improves response quality in summarization
L40:                                                       and single-turn dialogue while being substantially simpler to implement and train.

L187: 4   Direct Preference Optimization
L188: Motivated by the challenges of applying reinforcement learning algorithms on large-scale problems
L189: such as fine-tuning language models, our goal is to derive a simple approach for policy optimization
L190: using preferences directly. Unlike prior RLHF methods, which learn a reward and then optimize it
L191: via RL, our approach leverages a particular choice of reward model parameterization that enables
L192: extraction of its optimal policy in closed form, without an RL training loop. As we will describe
L193: next in detail, our key insight is to leverage an analytical mapping from reward functions to optimal
L194: policies, which enables us to transform a loss function over reward functions into a loss function
L195: over policies. This change-of-variables approach avoids fitting an explicit, standalone reward model,
L196: while still optimizing under existing models of human preferences, such as the Bradley-Terry model.
L197: In essence, the policy network represents both the language model and the (implicit) reward.
