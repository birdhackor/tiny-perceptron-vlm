`Counter.most_common` breaks equal counts by encounter order, which here means the fixed corpus/list iteration order. Ties in aba and bab select ab and ba respectively. The merger makes one left-to-right pass and advances i by 1 or 2 on each iteration. Every accepted match consumes two original positions, so it cannot reuse the second position in an overlapping match. Empty, singleton, nonmatching, final-position and already-merged inputs passed. For aaaa, the counter sees three overlapping aa pairs while merging yields only two aa tokens. These are different counts and the lesson's actual nonoverlapping ab example does not conflate them. An independent positional oracle checked 2,044 combinations of binary rows of length 0..8 and four character pairs; reconstruction and output length n minus accepted nonoverlapping matches were exact. Documents [a] and [b] were not joined into a pair across rows. For an entirely pair-free corpus the shown top-level most_common indexing would fail; this is outside its fixed toy inputs and is not claimed to be a complete tokenizer.

The reviewer-only finite-loop illustration uses the exact merger but is clearly separate from the lesson's one-step code: total token positions descend 8,5,4,3,2 and the symbol inventory grows 3,4,5,6,7.
