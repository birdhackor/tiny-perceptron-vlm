141: 3   Preliminaries
142: We review the RLHF pipeline in Ziegler et al. (and later [40, 1, 28]). It usually includes three phases:
143: 1) supervised fine-tuning (SFT); 2) preference sampling and reward learning and 3) RL optimization.
144: SFT: RLHF typically begins by fine-tuning a pre-trained LM with supervised learning on high-quality
145: data for the downstream task(s) of interest (dialogue, summarization, etc.), to obtain a model π SFT .
146: Reward Modelling Phase: In the second phase the SFT model is prompted with prompts x to
147: produce pairs of answers (y1 , y2 ) ∼ π SFT (y | x). These are then presented to human labelers
148: who express preferences for one answer, denoted as yw ≻ yl | x where yw and yl denotes the
149: preferred and dispreferred completion amongst (y1 , y2 ) respectively. The preferences are assumed
150: to be generated by some latent reward model r∗ (y, x), which we do not have access to. There are a
151: number of approaches used to model preferences, the Bradley-Terry (BT) [5] model being a popular
152: choice (although more general Plackett-Luce ranking models [32, 23] are also compatible with the
153: framework if we have access to several ranked answers). The BT model stipulates that the human
154: preference distribution p∗ can be written as:
