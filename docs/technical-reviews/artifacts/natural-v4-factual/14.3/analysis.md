## Mathematical derivation and numerical checks

For real nonzero vectors, `q·k = ||q||₂ ||k||₂ cos(theta)`. Holding the directions fixed while multiplying a key by a positive number multiplies its signed dot product by that number. An increase in the signed score requires positive cosine; an obtuse pair can instead become more negative. The section's scale comparison has two explicitly acute 45-degree pairs, so its length-increase wording applies to that concrete case. The probe also checks the antiparallel counterexample -1 -> -100, preventing a universal signed-monotonicity inference.

`q=[1,1]`, `k1=[1,0]`, `k2=[0,100]` give lengths `sqrt(2), 1, 100`; the two cosines are `1/sqrt(2)`, hence both angles are 45 degrees. The original dot products are 1 and 100. L2 normalization gives `qhat=[1/sqrt(2),1/sqrt(2)]`, `khat1=[1,0]`, `khat2=[0,1]`; both scores are `1/sqrt(2)=0.7071067811865475`. Their softmax probabilities are exactly 1/2 by symmetry.

`softmax(s)_i=exp(s_i)/sum_j exp(s_j)` is nonnegative and sums to one for finite real inputs and a nonempty candidate set. With exact `e=2.718281828459045`, `[1,0]` gives `[e/(e+1),1/(e+1)] = [0.7310585786300049,0.2689414213699951]`. The displayed 2.718 and 3.718 are approximations. `[1,100]` can be calculated after subtracting 100: `[exp(-99)/(1+exp(-99)),1/(1+exp(-99))]`, approximately `[1.0112214926104486e-43,1]`. The CPU float32 first entry is `1.0089348943138683e-43`, a subnormal rounding difference, not a mathematical zero. PyTorch's demonstrated output is finite; the exponential definition is not an instruction to implement naive float32 `exp(100)`.

With unequal scores `[1,0]`, scale 4 gives `[exp(4)/(1+exp(4)),1/(1+exp(4))] = [0.9820137900379085,0.01798620996209156]`. Division by positive temperature `T=.25` is the same operation. For fixed unequal scores and positive temperature, lower temperature concentrates probability around the maximum; ties remain tied. The independent probe verifies the original float32 output and reciprocal relation. Henry et al. Eq. (5) provides the separate learned scaling factor after L2 Q/K normalization; it does not turn the local demonstration into model-quality evidence.

The exercise replaces the second key by `[0,.1]`, keeping its direction and changing its positive length. Dot products become `[1,.1]`, softmax approximately `[0.7109495026250039,0.289050497374996]`. Its normalized key remains `[0,1]`, so the two normalized weights remain `.5`. IEEE float32 represents `.1` approximately; the check accounts for this difference.

`F.normalize` in the retrieved official PyTorch v2.14.1 source uses default `p=2` and `v/max(||v||₂,eps)`, with default `eps=1e-12`. Exact unit-vector/cosine statements require nonzero norms at least eps. Every section example satisfies this condition. The probe separately checks zero and a sub-epsilon vector: zero maps to zero, and `[1e-14,0]` maps to `[.01,0]`, so neither is treated as a unit direction. Positive rescaling preserves the direction in the section's exercise; arbitrary sign flips would not.
