Own mathematical derivation, completed before verdict; fixed Q/K content.
Let R(t)=[[cos(t),-sin(t)],[sin(t),cos(t)]]. Matrix columns are the rotated unit horizontal and vertical basis. R(t)[x,y]^T=[cos(t)x-sin(t)y,sin(t)x+cos(t)y]^T. cos²+sin²=1 makes R(t)^T R(t)=I, so squared length x²+y² is preserved.
For fixed q,k and angles aθ,bθ: (R(aθ)q)^T(R(bθ)k)=q^T R(aθ)^T R(bθ)k=q^T R((b-a)θ)k. Replacing a,b by a+s,b+s cancels s. With q=k=[1,0], this equals cos((b-a)θ). This is a real-number identity; float32 implementations agree within rounding tolerance.
θ=π/4=45°. b-a=5-2=15-12=25-22=3, hence135° and cos=-√2/2≈-0.7071067811865475, four decimals-0.7071. A common shift10 adds450° to each rotated vector. b-a=5-3=2 gives90°, cos=0 mathematically. b-a=26-22=4 gives180°, cos=-1. The two-dimensional lesson is periodic every8 positions; it is not claimed to uniquely encode arbitrarily large positions.
At45°, cos=sin=√2/2≈0.7071067811865475; at135° cos=-√2/2, sin=√2/2; at90° cos=0,sin=1. An angle in degrees timesπ/180 gives radians;180°→π and45°→π/4.

Orthogonality and shift cancellation hold pair by pair; each frequency is shared by Q and K for that pair. This does not preserve context-dependent Q/K after arbitrary re-encoding a longer sentence.
