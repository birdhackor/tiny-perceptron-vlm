Source derivation: scene defaults to size=16 and offset=0; colors has red=[1,0,0], blue=[0,0,1]; zeros(3,16,16) starts black. With indexing=ij, yy is height/row and xx width/column. size//2=8, size//4=4. The square mask is |x-8|<=4 and |y-8|<=4, so indices 4 through 12 are selected in each direction. (8,8) is selected; (0,0) is not. Thus red center [1,0,0], corner [0,0,0]; changing red to blue changes channel 2, not the number of images. There are 3*16*16=768 scalar values. Inserting a length-one axis leaves those values intact; stacking two images makes the leading axis length 2. Channel interpretation: independent planes share the same 16 by 16 coordinates. For this red square only channel 0 is nonzero. They can each be visualized as grayscale, but the three combined channel values identify one RGB pixel. A diagnostic 3 by 2 by 4 arange tensor yields [6,14,22] at [:,1,2], confirming equal row/column coordinates across channels. Batch [0] selects all channels and pixels from the first image; batch [0,:,8,8] selects that image's one RGB pixel.

Shape and range conditions: C,H,W is a convention of this interface, not an intrinsic tensor property. Official TorchVision to_tensor source accepts numpy/Pillow images, moves HWC into CHW, and divides uint8 images by 255 after floating-point conversion. Other dtypes are not universally divided. Pillow documents RGB as three 8-bit bands and separately lists other modes and depths. Therefore the lesson's 'often' integer 0..255 photos and 'can' floating 0..1 are appropriately conditional. scene is already float32 with 0..1 values; dividing again lowers 1 to approximately 0.003921568859368563. A change in scaling/order changes the numerical input received by a model; this is not a quantified recognition-accuracy claim. HWC->CHW needs a permutation; arbitrary reshape does not preserve the association of each color with each spatial coordinate. VisionEncoder's forward guard rejects the wrong HWC input layout.
